01. Discovery

User Interviews

5 women ranging from 25–53 years old were interviewed. The intimate interviews revealed varying perspectives of how a family was defined by different women.

"Maybe if my sister knew that a lot of the women in my family had post-partum depression, she could've prepared for it and not felt so alone."

Persona & Empathy Map

Current primary users are Gen Z and Millennial daughters along with baby boomer mothers, which were the main demographics whose relationships were found to be the most affected by technology.

02. Define

Problem Statement

Interviews pointed to one gap: women stop sharing family health history once they start protecting the next generation from worry. Right now that means important details slip through in a single conversation — an offhand comment, or a story told after the fact.

03. Ideate

Concept Sketches

Early sketches tested three directions: a shared family timeline, a private one-on-one journal, and a prompt-based Q&A. The prompt-based format tested best — it gave people permission to say things they wouldn't otherwise bring up unprompted.

04. UI

Visual Design

The interface leans on the same calm, editorial system as the rest of the site — soft neutrals, an ink-and-accent palette, and a serif/sans pairing that reads as thoughtful rather than clinical, since the subject matter already carries enough weight on its own.

05. Validate

Usability Testing

Five follow-up sessions with the original interviewees confirmed the prompt format lowered the barrier to sharing. Two participants said they answered a prompt about a health condition they'd never mentioned to their daughters before.
