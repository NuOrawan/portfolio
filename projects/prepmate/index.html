2025 — App Design

PrepMate

A mobile app that turns emergency preparedness from a daunting, fear-driven task into a structured, achievable process — guiding people through a personalized plan instead of a generic checklist.

Role
UX Research
Duration
8 weeks
Tools
FigJam, Figma
View Final Presentation
The Problem

How might we better prepare for hazards and disruptions during emergencies when professional responders are not immediately available?

Goal & Proposed Solution

Research & Goal

  • Simplify: Make emergency preparedness easy to understand and act on.
  • Motivate: Encourage proactive planning through personalized guidance and progress tracking.
  • Empower: Build user confidence and resilience during emergencies.

The Solution

A guided, step-by-step mobile experience — personalized preparedness plans, transparent progress tracking, and a clear design system — that turns overwhelming emergency prep into simple, doable actions.

The Process
01. Discovery 02. Define 03. Ideate 04. About the App 05. Design Process 06. Validate
01. Discovery

User Interviews

Conducted 8 user interviews to uncover preparedness needs, behaviors, and pain points, and confirmed the insights against feedback from Emergency Management staff and further research. Four themes stood out: information overload (people want to prepare but don't know where to start), difficulty sustaining readiness over time, the need for personalization, and preparedness working best when framed as an ongoing habit rather than a one-time task.

Persona & User Journey Map

Research surfaced two distinct archetypes: “Emergency Prep Newbies,” who feel overwhelmed by where to start, and “Doomsday Preppers,” who struggle to sustain and maintain plans they’ve already built.

Competitive Analysis

A review of existing preparedness and alert apps found they focus on alerts rather than actionable guidance, are built for experienced users rather than beginners, and rarely offer a personalized plan — gaps PrepMate was designed to close.

02. Define

Problem Statement

How might we better prepare people for hazards and disruptions during emergencies when professional responders aren’t immediately available?

03. Ideate

Concept Sketches

Using journey mapping and “How Might We” exercises, I explored ways to reduce user overwhelm and shape a more guided, personalized preparedness experience, generating concepts like personalized preparedness paths and bite-sized educational resources.

04. About the App

Overview

PrepMate is a mobile app designed to be a trusted guide in any emergency, turning disaster preparedness from a daunting, fear-driven task into a structured, attainable process. Its mission is to help people build resilience and get ready for moments when professional help may be delayed.

Who It’s For

PrepMate speaks to two research-backed archetypes — “Emergency Prep Newbies” who don’t know where to start, and “Doomsday Preppers” who need help sustaining the plans they already have.

05. Design Process

Visual Design

Through an iterative Figma process, I developed a modern, accessible UI focused on clarity and trust. Testing informed refinements to navigation, task flows, and information hierarchy, resulting in a more intuitive experience with reduced cognitive load. Three features anchor the experience: personalized onboarding with multiple paths to choose from, guided profile setup that generates a tailored emergency plan, and real-time alerts for hazards specific to the user’s location with clear next steps.

06. Validate

Usability Testing

What I wanted to learn: How easily can first-time users create a profile, find an emergency plan for their area, and set alerts? Where do they get stuck, and what would bring them back to the app?

Method: Moderated usability sessions, remote over Zoom and in person, with 3 participants of different ages and genders. None had used PrepMate before. I built three low-fidelity versions (Designs A, B and C) of each task flow, for nine prototypes in total.

Task completion: Of the 6 tasks attempted, 5 were completed without help and 1 was completed with assistance.

Findings by Task

1. Create a member profile. Participants found the steps easy to follow, and one liked seeing the total number of steps and their progress along the way. The most serious issue (high severity): onboarding sent everyone down a learning path, even people who opened the app for another reason. The profile also asked for more information than it needed (moderate).

2. Change location and view an emergency plan. The bottom menu kept the main sections within easy reach, and participants liked checking off supplies and being reminded when items expire. The labels were less clear (moderate): participants suggested “Prepare” instead of “Plan,” “Learn” instead of “To-do,” and “Your Supply List” instead of “Tool Kit.” It also wasn’t obvious that to-do items could be skipped (low).

3. Set alert preferences. Participants liked being able to set alerts for more than one location in Design B. But alert settings and live location monitoring felt separate from the main location setting, even though they belong together (moderate).

What I Changed

I replaced the single learning path with several starting options, so people can go straight to what they came for. I also renamed unclear labels, made it clear that steps can be skipped, and cut steps from alert setup by turning alerts on by default (each one can still be switched off) and removing an extra confirmation screen. I recommended using the current location by default and asking for less information during profile setup.
